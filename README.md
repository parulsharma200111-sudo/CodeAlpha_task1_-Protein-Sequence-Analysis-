# CodeAlpha_task1_-Protein-Sequence-Analysis-
This analysis used BLASTp to compare a partial Escherichia coli RecA protein sequence (Accession No. XNZ85062.1, length 169 amino acids) against the ClusteredNR protein database to identify homologous sequences and assess evolutionary conservation.
# Key Findings
  The results demonstrate extraordinary evolutionary conservation of the RecA protein's core domain, particularly among the bacterial taxa analyzed
| **Statistical Metric** | **Range of Results (Selected Hits)** | **Interpretation** |
|-------------------------|--------------------------------------|--------------------|
| **% Identity** | 95.08% to 100.00% | High identity confirms the sequence is largely unchanged across species, indicating a crucial, non-negotiable biological function. |
| **E-value** | 2e−66 to 9e−119 | Extremely low E-values indicate the alignments are highly statistically significant and are not due to chance, definitively proving homology (shared ancestry). |
| **Query Coverage** | 98% to 100% | Nearly the entire 169-amino-acid query sequence is conserved, confirming that this functional domain is universally present in the homologous proteins. |

# Evolutionary Significance
- **100% Identity:** Closely related species within the *Enterobacteriaceae* family (*E. fergusonii*, *E. coli*, *Klebsiella pneumoniae*) showed **100% sequence identity** for the query region, confirming them as **near-perfect homologs**.  
- **Strong Extended Homology:** More distantly related bacteria (*Pectobacterium actinidiae*, *Mixta calida*) still maintained a **very high identity (≥95%)**, reinforcing the idea that this protein’s structure is **intolerant of mutation**.  
- **Functional Conservation:** The strong conservation across diverse bacterial species indicates that the **RecA protein** — known for its vital role in **DNA repair and homologous recombination** — is a **highly essential protein** fundamental to bacterial survival.

# Conclusion
The BLAST analysis confirms that the RecA protein is a highly conserved molecular marker whose essential function in maintaining genomic integrity is preserved across a wide range of bacterial taxa.
